§0 · Charter

What this round is,
and what is out of bounds

The gap analysis closed the distance to a top-tier open-source project. The long-horizon proposals raised the ceiling. Everything in both — GAP·T1–T5, GAP·D1–D5, LH·F1–F5, LH·D1–D5, LH·C1–C10 — plus their recorded kill lists, is out of bounds here. So is the TUI, which a live redesign spec already owns.

What remains is the layer those documents could not reach from inside an engineering frame: identity, boundaries, strategy, subtraction, the economics of agent attention, and the cost of the project’s own inner loop. Ten proposals survived four adversarial rounds; thirteen did not, and their deaths are recorded so they aren’t re-litigated — a house tradition by now.

Two rules govern every entry. Adjacency is disclosed, never hidden — where an item borders prior work, the border is stated in the entry. And every measurable claim was measured, this session, from this working tree. When a number appears, a command produced it.

§1 · The Rubric

Six axes, two of them new

The long-horizon round scored on Leverage, Evidence, Differentiation, Agent-first, Durability, and Cost. This round keeps the spine and changes two axes, because this round admits documents as deliverables — and documents fail differently than features. They fail by arriving too late, or by never being read.

Dropped: Differentiation (for strategy work, differentiation is the content, not a score) and Agent-first (agents are the declared primary users; a separate axis double-counts what Leverage and Compounding already price). Added: Timing and Consumption.

AxisThe questionWhat a 5 looks like
LeverageHow many future decisions, workflows, and personas improve at once?Every later planning round and every session inherits it.
EvidenceIs it grounded in measured repo facts and the dogfood corpus, not vibes?Cites paths, counts, and commands run this session.
TimingIs now materially cheaper than later? Is there an expiry window?Post-v0.1.0 or post-adoption, the move becomes breaking or impossible.
CompoundingDoes the value accrue — especially against agent session amnesia?Converts re-derived-every-session knowledge into durable structure.
ConsumptionWho reads or runs this, when, and what breaks if they don’t?A gate, a template field, or a CI job — not a hope.
Cost⁻¹Plan + build + maintain burden, inverted.An afternoon to draft, near-zero to keep.

Each axis scores 1–5; items are ranked by Σ/30. Scores are ordinal, self-assigned, and argued in public in each entry — the Colophon states the biases.

§2 · The Gauntlet

Four rounds, four adversaries

  1. The Novelty Audit. Dies if the gap analysis, the long-horizon doc, or their kill lists already own it. Survivors must disclose every adjacency inline.
  2. The Shelfware Tribunal. Every document-shaped proposal names its reader and its enforcement mechanism, or dies. Who reads this, when, and what breaks if they don’t?
  3. Constraint & Timing. Must not violate the architecture’s invariants (git-native, no daemon, append-only, merge-conflict-free by construction) — and must answer why now, at v0.0.1, with zero external adopters.
  4. The Persona Gauntlet. Survivors face all four adversaries below; each entry records the sharpest objection and the argument that answered it.

The Year-Two Maintainer

Lives with whatever this round creates after the excitement wears off. Kills rot, ritual, and anything that needs a gardener.

The Cold-Start Agent

Wakes with no memory, pays for every token it reads, and believes documentation over reality. Kills context bloat and drift.

The Skeptical Adopter

Arrives from the README with GitHub Issues open in the next tab. Kills claims without evidence and paths that only work here.

The Rival Author

Building the competing tool; reads this repository for weaknesses. Kills undefended moats and unpriced risks.

01

The Armature Constitution

Survived R1–R4Σ 27

The project’s invariants are its sharpest asset, and they are currently buried on line 20 of an 1,867-line implementer document.

What

A versioned charter, deliberately small — ≤ 2 KB, because it must earn a seat in every agent session’s standing context — in three parts. Invariants, numbered, each phrased as we will / we will never: git-native; no daemon; append-only history is never rewritten; merge-conflict-free by construction; agents are the primary users; deterministic gates decide, LLM judges advise; done ≠ merged. Non-goals: not a CI system, not an agent framework, not a chat bus, not a merge authority, not a human-first PM suite. Tripwires — sentences that kill a proposal on contact: needs a long-running process; rewrites history; syncs bidirectionally with a mutable remote.

Evidence
  • The constraints exist — as “Key Constraints,” §1 of docs/design/architecture.md, an 1,867-line document whose stated audience is implementers and whose details have drifted from the live tree.
  • Both prior planning rounds had to reconstruct the constraint set by hand; the long-horizon doc invented a “constraint round” precisely because no citable boundary document exists.
  • The strongest precedent is the house’s own sentence: kills are recorded “so they aren’t re-litigated.” A constitution is that sentence promoted to standing policy.
Not that

GAP·T4.3 documents what the harness hook guarantees; architecture.md states constraints for implementers. This is neither — it is the small, stable, citable layer above both, with a consumption mechanism attached. The killed trust-model item (§3) survives here as a tripwire.

The red team

“A values poster. It will be beautiful, and it will be wallpaper.” — R2, the Year-Two Maintainer

Answered: the consumption mechanism is the proposal. An ADR-template field (“principles touched,” riding GAP·D5.2’s template work), kill rounds that cite tripwires by number instead of re-arguing them, and a hard token budget that keeps it inside AGENTS.md context. Wallpaper can’t be cited; this is built to be cited.

The plan produces

A one-page charter draft; ratification as an ADR; the "Principles touched" field added to the ADR template created by GAP·D5.2 (required, non-empty — invariant/tripwire IDs or none); a lint check enforcing that field is non-empty on every new ADR; the AGENTS.md wiring; an amendment bar (an ADR, not an edit). architecture.md's "Key Constraints" section (§1) is deleted and replaced with a one-line context pointer to CONSTITUTION.md — architecture.md may still elaborate on how an invariant is implemented, but never re-states what it is, so the two documents cannot drift apart. Sequenced after GAP·D5 — see docs/design/next-work-sequencing.md, pulled forward to Tier S so the template exists before the Constitution needs it.

The Subtractive Release

Survived R1–R4Σ 27

The cheapest deletion window this project will ever have is open now, and it closes at v0.1.0.

What

An evidence-based census of every user-facing surface — issue types, statuses, confidence states, fields, commands, flags. Each entry must show use in the dogfood corpus, or carry a written justification, or be cut before the first real release. Cut means parked, not purged: the surface leaves the product, stays in git history, and carries a one-paragraph re-entry criterion.

Evidence
  • Measured this session across 6,918 ops in 71 worker logs: the feature issue type appears zero times — while CONTEXT.md needs paired “Avoid” entries to keep Story and Feature apart.
  • A behavior type appears 8 times in the live corpus and zero times in CONTEXT.md — surface the glossary doesn’t know exists.
  • inferred confidence: no occurrences found; draft appears 414 times.
  • 46 top-level commands, including import, accept-citation, stale-review, scope-delete — usage unknown, cost permanent.
Not that

LH·D1 ran exactly this audit on config knobs — treat it as the completed pilot. LH·D4 (merged-promotion) and LH·D5 (dotdir) are single instances. This is the general sweep, with GAP·T5.3’s v0.1.0 release as the deadline that gives it teeth.

The red team

“You’ll cut something my team needs in month two.” — R4, the Skeptical Adopter

Answered: restoring a parked verb costs a release note. Carrying an unused one costs tests, docs, schema surface, and agent context — forever, for everyone. The asymmetry is the argument.

The plan produces

The census table (surface × corpus evidence × ruling); the park protocol; a PR series sequenced before v0.1.0; a standing surface-budget rule — every new verb starts with a census row.

03

Context Economics

Survived R1–R4Σ 26

Line 15 of the architecture document declares “minimizes context consumption for AI agents” a design goal. Nothing in the repository measures it.

What

Make tokens a governed budget the way coverage already is: a report target that prices every agent-facing artifact — skills, rendered contexts, --format agent payloads, the glossary — budgets per artifact class, a CI gate that fails when an artifact grows past budget, and a trim plan for the current offenders.

Evidence
  • The embedded skill surface totals 100,926 bytes (~25k tokens). The coordinator skill alone is 32,271 bytes (~8k tokens) — re-read by every coordinator session, every wave.
  • CONTEXT.md: 16,903 B. docs/commands.md: 21,531 B. concepts.md: 17,021 B. use-cases.md: 14,194 B.
  • The one existing knob — config token_budget — is documented as inert (LH·D1’s own evidence).
Not that

LH·C3 improves error quality; GAP·D2 fixes doc accuracy; GAP·T1.3’s pseudocode-to-subcommand moves are token wins it doesn’t count. This is the missing meter: weight, priced and gated. It is also what finally gives LH·D1’s token_budget knob a truthful meaning.

The red team

“Budgets get gamed. The prose gets terser and more ambiguous, and I will misread it at 3 a.m.” — R4, the Cold-Start Agent

Answered: the budget is paired with GAP·T1’s semantic skill tests. An artifact must pass the comprehension gate and the weight gate; compression that breaks the golden transcripts fails the other check. Two gates, opposite failure modes.

The plan produces

A make context-report spec; the token-counting method; class budgets and a ratchet policy; the coordinator-skill trim as the first application.

04

The Paved Road

Survived R1–R4Σ 25

Forty-six commands, four deployment personas — and no sentence anywhere that says: this is the way.

What

Declare one blessed end-to-end pipeline — bootstrap → plan/decompose → wave dispatch → work → review → sync — as product truth. Documentation leads with it; defaults optimize for it; skills teach only it; everything else becomes an escape hatch, labeled as such in --help. The census (№ 02) takes paved-road membership as its first sorting criterion.

Evidence
  • 46 commands in five help groupings offer many workflows and bless none.
  • LH·D4 found three coexisting merged-promotion mechanisms with contradicting docs — the signature failure of an unopinionated surface.
  • The session-recovery-gaps theme records a coordinator abandoning the worker protocol when reconstruction got expensive. When the road isn’t paved, agents go off-road.
Not that

GAP·D1.1 rewrites the quickstart so it is accurate; this decides which workflow deserves to be the quickstart. LH·C8’s dispatch policy is one paved-road brick; this is the road.

The red team

“Opinions in a doc don’t bind anything.” — R2, the Shelfware Tribunal

Answered with three bindings: the CI-executed quickstart (GAP·D1.1) is the paved road by definition; the skills document nothing else; off-road commands carry an explicit label in help output. Opinion becomes configuration.

The plan produces

The pipeline named step-by-step; the escape-hatch labeling policy; a doc/skill restructure list; a defaults audit — which flags exist only because the road was never chosen.

The CLI Grammar Contract

Survived R1–R4Σ 25

Every rename after v0.1.0 is a breaking change; every inconsistency kept is a permanent tax on agents.

What

A grammar specification — command taxonomy (groups get subcommands; no new hyphen compounds), flag conventions, the --format contract, an exit-code table, a TTY policy — plus an audit of all 46 commands against it, a rename-and-alias schedule that lands before v0.1.0, and a conformance test every new command must pass.

Evidence — all from arm --help, today
  • merged — a status used as a verb — sits beside transition.
  • sources is a proper command group; source-link is a hyphenated orphan beside it.
  • scope-delete and scope-rename got hyphens where review got subcommands.
  • stale-review beside review; worker-init beside workers; push-ops beside sync.
Not that

LH·C3 governs what comes back (errors); this governs what you type. LH·D4 consolidates one redundant verb; this is the law that stops the next one from appearing.

The red team

“Renames now break the dogfood fleet’s muscle memory and every published skill.” — R3, Constraint & Timing

Answered: the fleet has no muscle memory, only context — and skills are single-sourced in internal/skillsembed/, regenerated in one commit. That is exactly why the window is pre-adoption. Old names alias for one release, then die — LH·D3’s shim policy, applied at birth instead of at the funeral.

The plan produces

The grammar spec; the 46-command audit table; the rename map with alias sunset dates; a conformance-test spec at the command-registration layer.

Every one of the 69 findings that justify this roadmap was collected on one Go monorepo, by one maintainer, mostly on one harness.

What

A deliberate dogfood campaign on a foreign repository — different language and shape (a TypeScript service, say), ideally a second harness — running one full story arc down the paved road, capturing findings through the existing pipeline, and ending in a generality report: which assumptions traveled, and which were Armature-shaped all along.

  • Go-specific residue is already leaking into product design: LH·F1 bakes gopls and go.work mitigations into the managed-worktree proposal.

The red team

07

Tiered Quality Gates

Survived R1–R4Σ 25

The commit gate runs the full test suite twice and mutation-tests thirty-one packages — on every commit, and again on every push to every branch.

What

Keep every threshold; move when each is paid. Inner loop, in seconds: lint plus tests for changed packages. Commit gate: the full suite once — the coverage profile taken from the same run instead of a second one. Merge gate, in CI: full suite plus coverage. Nightly: the full mutation sweep, with a ratchet — a red nightly blocks next-day merges via required status. Step one of the plan is measuring current wall-clock, the number nobody has written down.

Evidence
  • make check = lint + go test ./... + go test -coverprofile ./... — the suite compiles and runs twice — + gremlins across ./internal (31 packages, 95% mutation coverage / 99% efficacy) + build.
  • quality-gates.md: “must pass before every commit/push.” ci.yml repeats the identical pipeline on push to '**'.
  • Every fleet worker pays this toll per task; so does every human commit.
Not that

Neither prior document touches pipeline latency — they add CI work (GAP·T1.1, GAP·D1.1, GAP·T4.1). This item is what keeps those additions affordable.

The red team

“Nightly mutation is mutation you can ignore.” — R4, the Year-Two Maintainer, prosecuting his own witness

Answered: the ratchet. A red nightly blocks merges the next morning exactly as a red commit gate would have. What changes is who waits — a scheduled job instead of every worker on every task — not whether anyone does.

The plan produces

The wall-clock baseline; a tier assignment per gate; the Makefile and CI restructure spec; the nightly ratchet policy; a fleet-throughput before/after estimate.

The Harness Compatibility Contract

Survived R1–R4Σ 24

The enforcement layer rides third-party hook APIs that can change any Tuesday, and the support claim is a comma-separated list in an architecture doc.

What

An adapter seam — one internal interface the harness hook targets per platform — plus a public support matrix (harness × capability: enforce / advisory / none), versioned and tested; a canary CI job against latest harness releases, scheduled and initially non-blocking; and an entry/exit policy for harness support.

Evidence
  • architecture.md §1: “Compatible with Claude Code, Gemini CLI, Cursor, Windsurf, Kiro” — no per-harness capability statement, no test behind the sentence.
  • Three legacy shims already live at v0.0.1 (LH·D3’s census) — each one the fossil of an untracked harness format change.
  • Skills already fan out to three harness directories: .claude/, .gemini/, .codex/.
Not that

GAP·T4.1 tests our hook’s allow/deny logic; GAP·T4.3 documents today’s guarantees honestly; LH·D3 buries yesterday’s shims. This governs tomorrow’s: the seam decides where future migrations live, so shims stop accreting in the security-relevant path.

The red team

“This is T4.3 plus D3 wearing a trenchcoat.” — R1, the Novelty Audit — the hardest round for this item

Answered: neither defines forward policy. T4.3 is a snapshot of today’s guarantees; D3 is a funeral for old code. Nothing anywhere says what happens when Claude Code changes its hook payload next month — and that gap is where the next three shims come from.

The plan produces

The adapter interface sketch; support matrix v1; the canary CI design with a promotion path from advisory to blocking; the harness add/drop policy.

The Strategy Memo

Survived R1–R4Σ 23

Three roadmap documents now exist, and none of them says who this is for first.

What

One internal memo that decides: the wedge — a named first persona; the evidence points at the solo operator running an agent fleet on a real repository. Scored lowest of the ten on Consumption for exactly this reason; kept because every other item’s sequencing quietly assumes an answer to the question it decides.

§7 · Colophon

Method honesty. The rubric is self-scored by the same author who proposed the items — the same bias the long-horizon round carried, inherited knowingly. Scores are ordinal, not cardinal: a 27 is not “12.5% better” than a 24; it is earlier in line. The red-team voices are internal adversarial passes, not independent reviewers; their value is that every objection recorded here was strong enough to kill three of this document’s earlier drafts’ candidates.

The document renders in light and dark, prints acceptably, and depends on no network resource — like the project it describes, it is a single self-contained artifact.
