Armature · Architecture Review

Driver or Seam

Eight weeks of dogfooding says prompted agents cannot be trusted with facts. That is not the same as saying they cannot be trusted to drive — and the difference is the whole decision.
124 raw findings · 17 curated themes · 1,190 commits · 677 closed issues Adversarially verified 2026-08-23
The answer
No. A prompted LLM is not the wrong architecture — but prompted has to stop being load-bearing for facts.

The corpus proves one sentence: no governance step may be optional-by-prompt. Every step that depended on an agent choosing to type a command, pass a flag, run a full gate, or stand in the right checkout failed — repeatedly, under explicit contrary instructions, across every model tier.

That is an argument about who is allowed to assert a fact, not about who invokes the CLI. Rebuilding armature as a harness-launching driver would answer a question the evidence is not asking, at a cost you have already paid once.

01

Why this keeps coming back

You have flip-flopped once already, and the record of that reversal is the most useful evidence in the repository.

In May 2026 armature was the deterministic driver. arm orchestrate and arm worker run shipped with launch adapters for Claude, Codex and Devin, crash-resume state derivation, and a zero-trust diff/reset/stage/commit pipeline. Four weeks later the entire subsystem was deleted.

The reason recorded in docs/archive/orchestration/2026-06-03-remove-managed-execution-design.md is exactly one thing, and it is worth quoting because it is not what you remember it being:

“managed execution was abandoned because harness behavior and inversion of control created excessive complexity” 2026-06-03 removal design, lines 294–295 — the file's only stated rationale

I searched that document for api key, subscription, billing, market and cost. There are no matches. The addressable-market argument that you remember as the reason is not written down anywhere in the repository. It exists only in your head and now in this report — which means the next person to reopen this question, including a future you, will read "complexity" and conclude the constraint has lifted.

That is the first concrete finding: if the commercial constraint is real, it needs to be a recorded decision, not a memory.

02

What 124 findings actually say

The headline is not the one the frustration predicts. LLM misbehaviour is a minority of the pile — armature's own bugs are more than double it.

That number cuts against rebuilding the driver, but it understates the severity problem, because the LLM failures are concentrated in the worst tier. Restricting to the 29 findings rated P0 or P1 across the curated themes produces the single most decisive figure in this review.

One correction to my own analysis
An earlier draft of this review claimed a driver fails to prevent "62 of 124" findings. That divides by the wrong denominator — 18 of the 124 are positive findings recording things that worked, where the question is null. Against the 106 real problems a driver prevents 35 fully and 25 partially: 57%, a majority, not a minority. The honest statement is the P0/P1 one above, not the raw count.

The trend that makes it feel like pushing a rope

The subjective experience is real and it is measurable. As the prompted multi-agent workflow became the sole driver of development, the share of commits that are fixes rose almost monotonically.

The failure that should decide it

ARCHIMP-S15 stood recorded as merged — the status the constitution defines as confirmed-on-main — for code that never existed on main. Two agent-typed transition ops, twelve minutes after claim, promoted transitively by rollup. arm validate reported 730/730 clean over it. It was found months later, by accident, during unrelated branch cleanup.

I verified the mechanism in source rather than taking the finding's word for it:

  • cmd/armature/transition.go carries no git-evidence check on the merged path; --pr is optional by its own help text.
  • internal/materialize/engine.go:589 — RunRollup promotes parents by aggregating children's status, never verifying it.
  • internal/sync/sync.go:17 — DetectMerges skips issues with an empty branch and silently continues on error, then the caller prints “No merged branches detected.”

The P0 is structural, not a one-off. But the base rate matters too, and it is the strongest thing that can be said for the status quo: a content-based audit of all 569 merged records found 562 confirmed, 0 other false merges, 7 indeterminate. One bad family in ~570 — and because history is append-only, it was corrected by appending a cancellation rather than rewriting anything.

03

You already have a driver

This is the reframing that dissolves the flip-flop, and it came out of the adversarial pass arguing against the recommendation.

Armature runs a state machine today: survey the DAG, dispatch a wave, integrate outcomes, validate citations, close with a PR. That loop is real, it is load-bearing, and it is written in markdown and executed by an LLM.

The doc-drift finding cluster is what that costs: orchestration logic encoded as untestable prose and shell pseudocode drifted from the CLI, and from itself, repeatedly. That entire failure class has no analogue when the loop is compiled Go with tests.

The real question was never “driver or no driver.” It is whether the driver you already run is written in a language with a compiler and tests, or in prose that provably drifts.

Once you separate the two things that were bundled together in arm orchestrate, the decision stops being a reversal:

CapabilityStatusWhy
Launching harnessesStays deadVendor adapters, auth resolution, retry policy — verbatim the “Removed Configuration” of the 2026-06-03 design. Collides with N2, forces API-key billing, and is what actually produced the complexity that killed it.
Owning the loopBring into codeNothing in the removal design forbids arm owning branch derivation, transition authority, or running the gate itself. That document forbids supervising harnesses, which is a different thing.

Moving control-plane logic out of skill prose into refusing verbs is not a step back toward arm orchestrate. It is the thing you have been doing since ADR 0003, and the constitution has been quietly ratifying it the whole time.

04

What survived the attack

The verdict was drafted, then attacked from three independent lenses — one steelmanning the driver, one steelmanning seams, one auditing the constitution. Several attacks landed. These are the ones that changed the answer.

Attack held
The commercial case against the driver was misattributed — and it is the weakest evidence in the review

The draft cited the removal design as recording an addressable-market rationale. It does not; I verified this. Separately, the --print policy finding is medium confidence, sourced to two GitHub issue numbers and an unlinked policy note.

Effect: the commercial argument is real as your stated reason, but it is not documented history and should not be scored as an independent refutation. The driver's rejection stands on the ORCRMV record and the N2 collision alone — which is where it should have rested.

Attack held
“Derivable” is not “derived” — the invocation problem survives the recommendation

The plan makes merged derivable from git content. But the corpus contains the controlled experiment: that content-based audit already existed and found the phantom merge only because a human happened to run it, months late. The theme's own conclusion — “we cannot rely on agents to run commands, even if we ask them to” — indicts the verb as much as the flag.

Effect: real, and it is the strongest surviving argument for a driver. The answer is that unattended invocation is a packaging question — cron, CI, or a thin runner you own — not a constitutional one. The constitution constrains armature, not your crontab.

Attack held
The plan's own break-glass reintroduces the hole it exists to close

The draft proposed gating arm merged with --i7-override --reason as the bypass. ADR 0016 line 69 says verbatim: “Break-glass is a Release Override, not a flag on the agent verb.” This repo already has the proof — --force was typed reflexively on 11 consecutive claims.

Effect: corrected in the plan below. Break-glass must require a controlling terminal, record the reason, and never report green.

Attack held
Ordering error: the third value is a precondition, not a follow-on

A derived promoter without a not-checked value must render every indeterminate case as a confident two-valued answer — reproducing the exact DetectMerges defect it is meant to fix. The draft acknowledged this in its own risk list and then sequenced it second anyway.

Effect: stages reordered below.

Attack fell
“Enforcement can't reach a model typing arm transition directly”

Raised as fatal to the seam case. It is wrong, and I verified why: internal/harnesshook/evaluator.go:44 already inspects Bash command strings and returns DecisionBlock on direct git commit, with careful flag-skipping parsing. Command-pattern denial at PreToolUse is shipped, working machinery. Extending it to arm transition --to merged is a small change to an existing function.

Residual: the parser tests fields[0] == "git", so sh -c 'git commit' slips past. The bypass is narrower than claimed but not zero — which is exactly why enforcement belongs in the verb, with the hook as defence in depth.

Attack held
Hook-based mandates are Claude-shaped, and you ship to three harnesses

AGENTS.md deploys skills to .claude/, .gemini/ and .codex/ alike. Any rule enforced by a hook is advisory on two of three harnesses — the governing rule failing on the majority of the surface.

Effect: every mandate must reduce to a refusal inside an arm verb to be portable. Hooks are an accelerator, never the primary control. This is also ADR 0016's own stated position.

Attack held
Deriving merged quietly removes the last human attestation

I7 says accountability for merges and overrides never transfers to the system. A human merging a PR is accountable for the merge; once promotion is derived by code, nobody is accountable for the record — which is the artifact I6 governs and the one that was fiction in ARCHIMP-S15.

Effect: the plan needs a named human acceptance point — a doctor-surfaced derived-vs-recorded diff someone clears — or it trades an agent-authored false record for a program-authored one at larger blast radius.

05

What to actually do

Write the governing rule down first, because it settles every later argument: agents may drive work; they may not own facts.

STAGE 0
Stop recording fiction
Days, not weeks. Pure git facts, no LLM in the loop, no new architecture.
  • Introduce the third value — checked-true / checked-false / not-checked — and render not-checked as not-checked everywhere. This moved up from Stage 2: it is a precondition for anything derived.
  • arm transition --to merged refuses without content-on-main evidence.
  • RunRollup verifies rather than aggregates.
  • A doctor check: for every merged issue, is its declared scope's content actually on origin/main? This would have caught ARCHIMP-S15 on any day of the two months it went unnoticed.
  • Fix SkippedDeliveryGate: false — never write a positive assertion for a check that did not run.
STAGE 1
Close the loop without an agent typing anything
  • Persist the derived branch on the claim op, mirroring the shipped worktree_path precedent — and stop asking agents for --branch. Zero ops carried it across an entire story; no skill sentence will change that.
  • arm sync decides merged from git evidence including squash merges, pull-only, and reports what it did not examine alongside its verdict.
  • Gate arm merged on the presence — never the rating — of a conformance assessment, ordered before worktree teardown so the command that finds the gap does not also destroy the recovery window.
  • Break-glass is an ADR-0016 Release Override: controlling terminal, recorded reason, never green. Not a flag on an agent verb.
  • Add the human acceptance point: a derived-vs-recorded diff surfaced by doctor that a person clears, so I7 stays honest.
STAGE 2
Fix the seams that lie
These are what train agents to distrust gates, and a driver would inherit every one.
  • D8 confined to paths the repo could plausibly own — it currently flags harness-injected bind mounts as violations, “always red” across three recurrences.
  • CleanTree honours .gitignore, so running the mandatory gate stops failing the gate.
  • The overlap checker gets transitive closure, parent containment and glob matching, so --force stops being reflex.
  • Extend the shipped command-denial in evaluator.go to state-transition verbs, as defence in depth behind the verb-level refusal.
STAGE 3
A pre-registered decision point, not an open question
After Stages 0–2 have one full story of data, re-evaluate against a single criterion agreed now: does a new class of protocol failure appear that cannot be closed by a refusal at a verb?
  • If yes — write the amending ADR for a re-entrant arm run --once step function: foreground, human-launched, exits between steps, state only in git ops. Never a supervisor, never a daemon, and never model invocation inside arm. Note that the removal design's non-goals name run and dispatch explicitly, so this needs an amendment to that document too, not just to N2.
  • If no — the ORCRMV record stands and this question is closed by evidence rather than by fatigue.
The part most likely to be skipped
Take the candidate follow-up lists from all seventeen themes and file them as one tracked epic with a burn-down. The record is unambiguous that follow-up lists are where P0s go to wait — that is exactly how ARCHIMP-S15 survived two months. Without this, the plan degrades into the status quo with better intentions.
Keep this exactly as it is
The layered LLM review cascade — fix, then a deliberately broader review, then implement — is the most-confirmed working pattern in your entire corpus, across 10+ runs. It has caught P0 bugs that deterministic gates structurally could not see, including a bug inside one of your own shipped deterministic seams. Nothing in this plan replaces it. Make the whole-story holistic pass required rather than optional — and note that doing so blocks delivery on review, which ADR 0005 phase-one forbids, so it needs its own narrow amending ADR.
06

What would change this answer

Stated in advance, so the next reversal has to clear a bar rather than a mood.

SignalWhat it would mean
Unattended is the productIf the target is genuinely no-human-at-the-keyboard fleets, invocation-dependent control is insufficient and the driver case strengthens sharply. Answer this before Stage 3. The evidence cannot settle it; only you can.
Fix-per-seam stays flatThe delivery gate cost 38 fix commits to 3 feat and was bypassed three ways in nine days. If that ratio does not fall as control-plane authority consolidates, the whack-a-mole reading wins and ORCRMV's complexity verdict deserves revisiting on its own terms.
Cheap-model fleetsEvery fabrication instance in the corpus involved a haiku-tier worker. If the protocol-failure class is model-capability-bound, a driver is permanent structural cost for a temporary problem. If you are deploying cheap fleets deliberately, it is permanent.
A verb-proof failure appearsThe Stage 3 trigger. One class that no refusal-at-a-verb can close, and the amendment gets written.
07

How this was produced

Eight independent analysts read the corpus in parallel: two over the seventeen curated themes, two classifying all 124 raw findings file by file, one over 1,190 commits, one over 677 closed issues, one inventorying harness enforcement seams, one auditing the constitution and ADRs. A synthesis stage drafted the verdict; three adversarial reviewers then attacked it from fixed opposing lenses.

Every load-bearing claim in this report was re-verified directly against source rather than trusted from an analyst — the phantom merge and its three enabling code paths, the ORCRMV deletion commits, the fix-share trend, the 2,932-vs-10,620 line comparison, the shipped command-denial, and the absence of any commercial rationale in the removal design. Two analyst claims did not survive that check and are corrected in the text above.

That verification step is not incidental. It is the same discipline this report recommends: a fluent assertion is not evidence, and the fix is to derive the fact rather than to ask more firmly.
